From January 24 to 26, 2026, I was in Singapore for AAAI-26, where I gave an oral presentation of our paper at the 16th AAAI Symposium on Educational Advances in Artificial Intelligence (EAAI): <em>How does LLM-powered coding assistance shape incidental learning? Exploring cognitive forcing strategies in programming education</em>. Ours was the only student group from the University of Dayton at the conference.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/2026aaai/aaai.jpg" class="img-fluid rounded" caption="At the AAAI-26 lobby." %}

## LeetCoach

Under the supervision of Dr. [Ngoc Nguyen](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ngocnt/), I worked with [Patrick Thomas](https://www.linkedin.com/in/patrickwthomas/) and [Quang Nguyen](https://www.linkedin.com/in/quangnguyen6/) to build LeetCoach, a full-stack coding practice platform. It runs offline, open-source LLMs that give learners guidance on problems instead of handing them the answer. We designed and built it before LeetCode released Leet.

On the system side, the backend is a set of microservices that communicate through a message queue, so requests are processed asynchronously. This keeps latency low when many users practice at the same time.

On the learning side, we asked how guided interactions based on cognitive forcing strategies affect incidental learning. We ran a controlled user study that measured learning outcomes and interaction patterns in the AI-assisted coding environment.
