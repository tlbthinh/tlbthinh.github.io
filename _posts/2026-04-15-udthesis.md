I defended my M.S. thesis, <em>PRISM: Predictive Review Integrity Scoring via Multimodality</em>, at the University of Dayton. My committee was Prof. Tam Nguyen (chair), Dr. James Buckley, and Prof. Tom Ongwere.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/2026thesisdefend/1.jpg" class="img-fluid rounded" caption="After my thesis defense." %}

## The problem

Fake reviews used to be written from scratch. With generative AI, people now take a real review and polish it: a language model makes the text more enthusiastic, and an image editing model makes a product defect look worse or a casual photo look professional. The review keeps a kernel of truth, which lets it slip past spam detectors built for fully fabricated content. Most of those detectors also read only the text, while e-commerce reviews often rely on photos as evidence.

## What I built

I built a dataset of about 40,000 reviews from the Amazon Reviews 2023 corpus. Reviews from 2021 and earlier form the human-written set, since they predate ChatGPT. For the AI-polished set, I took reviews from 2022 onward, rewrote their text with an LLM and edited their photos with a two-step pipeline: Qwen-3.5 wrote an editing instruction based on the review, and LongCat-Image applied it.

PRISM then picks its models in two stages. Stage 1 compares four text encoders (GPT-2, ELECTRA, RoBERTa, DistilBERT). Stage 2 pairs the best one with three image encoders (ViT, ResNet, EfficientNet) and combines their features.

## What I found

Adding images improved detection in every multimodal combination I tested. Text and images catch different traces of AI polishing, so a text-only detector misses part of the picture.

The model with the best AUC-ROC was not the best detector. GPT-2 scored the highest AUC-ROC on text (0.9707) but had the lowest recall (0.6659), so it would miss one in three AI-polished reviews. ELECTRA caught 91.6% of them, and became the text encoder for the multimodal stage.

Text alone has a ceiling. On real reviews from before ChatGPT existed, the text model still flagged about 23% as AI-polished, because some people simply write well. The image side is what helps separate those reviewers from polished reviews.
